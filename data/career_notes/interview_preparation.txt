Interview Preparation Guide

Behavioural interviews
Most companies use the STAR format: Situation, Task, Action, Result.
Prepare five to eight stories from your experience that can be adapted to common
questions: a time you dealt with conflict, a time you failed and learned, your biggest
achievement, a time you had to influence someone without authority.
Strong answers are specific and use numbers where possible ("the team missed the deadline
by two weeks" not "we had some delays").

Technical preparation
- For software engineering: review data structures (arrays, linked lists, trees, graphs,
  hashmaps), sorting algorithms, recursion, dynamic programming basics. Practice on
  LeetCode -- aim for ~50-100 easy/medium problems before interviews at large companies.
- For data roles: practice SQL (joins, aggregations, window functions, subqueries),
  Python/pandas for data wrangling, and be able to talk through a statistics concept
  such as A/B testing, p-values, or confidence intervals clearly.
- For ML roles: understand the bias-variance trade-off, regularization, gradient descent,
  and be ready to design a model evaluation strategy for a new problem.

System design preparation (for mid-senior roles)
Practice walking through designs out loud. Use a consistent structure:
  1. Clarify requirements and constraints (scale, latency, consistency needs)
  2. Estimate capacity (users, QPS, storage)
  3. Design the high-level architecture (services, databases, queues)
  4. Dive deep on one or two key components
  5. Discuss trade-offs and what you would do differently at larger scale

Common mistakes
- Not asking clarifying questions before jumping into a coding problem.
- Writing code without explaining your thinking aloud during a live interview.
- Giving vague behavioural answers ("we worked hard and figured it out").
- Negotiating salary too early in the process, before an offer is on the table.
- Not following up with a thank-you note or email after the interview.

Day-of tips
- Sleep well the night before.
- Have two or three questions ready to ask the interviewer (about the team,
  the tech stack, or current challenges).
- If you get stuck on a coding problem, narrate your thought process -- partial
  credit exists, and interviewers are assessing how you think.
