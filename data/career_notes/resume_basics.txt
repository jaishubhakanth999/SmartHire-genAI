Resume Writing Basics

A resume should be tailored to the specific role, not a single generic
document sent everywhere. Start with a short professional summary (2-3
sentences) that names the target role and your strongest relevant
qualification.

Use bullet points under each role, and lead each bullet with a strong verb
(built, led, reduced, automated) rather than a passive description of duties.
Where possible, quantify the impact: "reduced page load time by 40%" is far
stronger than "improved website performance."

List skills that are actually evidenced elsewhere in the resume -- a skills
section full of buzzwords with no supporting experience reads as padding to
both recruiters and applicant-tracking systems.

Keep formatting simple and consistent: one font, clear section headers, and
no more than two pages for most early- to mid-career candidates. Complex
tables, columns, or graphics can break automated resume parsers, which is
part of why this project extracts structured fields directly with an LLM
instead of relying on fragile layout-based parsing.

Proofread for consistent tense (past roles in past tense, current role in
present tense) and for consistent date formatting throughout.
