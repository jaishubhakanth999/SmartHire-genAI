Resume Writing Fundamentals

A resume is a marketing document, not a biography. Its one job is to get you a phone
screen. Keep that goal in mind for every decision.

Length and format
One page for under five years of experience. Two pages is acceptable for senior roles.
Never three pages. Use a clean, readable font (10-12 pt), consistent margins, and plain
section headings. Avoid tables, columns, and graphics if you are applying through an
Applicant Tracking System (ATS) -- they often scramble the parsing.

Section order (standard)
1. Contact info (name, city/country, phone, email, LinkedIn URL, GitHub if relevant)
2. Professional summary (2-3 lines, optional but useful for career changers)
3. Skills (a short list of tools and technologies)
4. Experience (most recent first, reverse-chronological)
5. Education
6. Certifications / projects (if relevant)

Writing strong bullet points
Use the formula: [Action verb] + [what you did] + [measurable result].
Weak:   "Responsible for building dashboards."
Strong: "Built 4 Power BI dashboards tracking weekly sales KPIs, reducing
         manual reporting time by 3 hours per week."

Skills section
List only skills you can defend in an interview. Group them logically:
Languages, Frameworks, Databases, Tools, Platforms.
Do not list Microsoft Word or "fast learner" as skills.

Tailoring for each application
Read the job description carefully and mirror its language where your experience
genuinely overlaps. ATS systems score resumes on keyword matches before a human
sees them. Tailor the skills section and the summary for each role -- the experience
bullets themselves can stay the same.

Common mistakes
- Typos and grammar errors (spell-check is not enough; read it aloud).
- Responsibilities listed instead of accomplishments.
- Dates missing or formatted inconsistently.
- Contact email that sounds unprofessional.
- Listing every technology you have ever touched instead of the ones you know well.
