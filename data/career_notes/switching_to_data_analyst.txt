Switching Into a Data Analyst Role

Data analyst roles typically expect comfort with SQL, spreadsheet or
dataframe-based analysis (Excel or pandas), one visualization tool (Power BI,
Tableau, or a plotting library), and basic statistics -- means, distributions,
correlation versus causation, and simple hypothesis testing.

If you are switching from an unrelated field, the fastest credible path is
usually: (1) learn SQL well enough to write joins and aggregations
comfortably, (2) build two or three small end-to-end projects using public
datasets that mirror the kind of questions the target industry asks, (3)
learn to present findings clearly, since communicating an insight matters as
much as finding it, and (4) reframe existing experience in analytical terms
-- for example, "tracked and reported weekly sales performance" is a data
analysis story even if the original job title was not "analyst."

A portfolio of a few well-documented projects (with a clear question, the
data source, the method, and the conclusion) is usually more convincing to
hiring managers than a certificate alone, though certificates can help pass
initial resume screens.

Common interview formats include a SQL screen, a take-home dataset analysis,
and a walkthrough of a past project where you explain your reasoning, not
just your final chart.
